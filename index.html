Learn Physical AI & Robotics with Phoebe

A warehouse row records something that already happened. When a model's output moves a physical arm, every grasp is a prediction and an experiment at once: the data depends on the policy that collected it, the label is a person driving the robot, the camera's position is part of every row, and the real test costs a person resetting the table each time, so you get tens of trials, not millions. Wrenfield Lab, a constructed team teaching a two-arm robot to pick and pack parcels, runs through every session: a policy that scores 100 percent in simulation and grasps 29 of 50 real parcels, then 49 of 50 once lighting and camera are randomized and 20 real demonstrations are mixed in, and 0 of 50 after the camera moves 2 cm without anyone recording it. Six leader sessions on the decision, ten practitioner sessions on the build. The research is cited; every Wrenfield number is constructed and computed in the page. Nothing here is investment advice or a view on any company.

If you come from a data team

Leader session 6 in three columns. What a conventional analyst, engineer or scientist already has that is worth more here, what is new, and what is actively dangerous.

Carries over

  • Data contracts and quality checks, pointed at episodes: clock, cameras, action, identity, outcome
  • Slicing a metric by segment, now by lighting, position and initial condition
  • Confidence intervals and honest comparisons, on tens of trials instead of millions of rows
  • Lineage: which version of what produced this row, now which robot, calibration and policy

Must learn

  • Episodes: observations and actions on one clock, and which column is the label
  • Coordinate frames and calibration: a camera's position is part of every row
  • Demonstrations and teleoperation: the label is a person driving the arm
  • Simulation and domain randomization, and checking it on the real robot

Must unlearn

  • "The data records the world": a robot's data records what its policy did
  • "More rows fix it": some gaps, like a lens the simulator lacks, need real data
  • "98 percent is a result": without the trial count it is not; 18 of 20 against 15 of 20 is p = 0.41
  • "Config is not data": an unrecorded 2 cm camera move took 49 of 50 to 0
