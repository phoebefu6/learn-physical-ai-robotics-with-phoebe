# Official course map - learn-physical-ai-robotics-with-phoebe

"Physical AI & Robotics Data", by Phoebe Fu. Bucket emrg, two-track: Leader a1-a6, Practitioner p1-p10
(p10 is the bench). 45 minutes per session. Skeleton approved by Phoebe 2026-11-22.
Internal build document: never linked from an audience page.

Angle: a data/AI course on the industry, never a tech tour. What changes when the model's output moves a
physical arm. Owns manipulation data: demonstrations, embodiment, calibration, small-trial evaluation,
sim-to-real.

Running case: **Wrenfield Lab**, a CONSTRUCTED robotics team teaching a two-arm robot to pick and pack
parcels. (The brief proposed "Tidewater Lab"; "Tidewater Grocers" is already learn-gcp's running case, so
the case was renamed. "Wrenfield" grepped clean across every learn-* course on 2026-11-22.) Every Wrenfield
number is generated by the seeded engine `assets/pai-live.js` and is labelled constructed wherever it
appears. No real company, product, vendor, robot brand, roadmap or incident appears anywhere. Research
papers are cited by author and year. Not investment advice (landing page and a1 say so).

**Re-verify before delivery.** Fast-moving field: dataset sizes, paper versions (several arXiv papers below
were revised in 2025) and the ISO 10218 revision status change. Contested timelines (when general-purpose
robots arrive) are taught as a disagreement; no page gives a date.

## 2. Verified facts

### F1. Domain randomization (Tobin et al. 2017, read at source, arXiv 1703.06907 full text)
Tobin, Fong, Ray, Schneider, Zaremba, Abbeel, "Domain Randomization for Transferring Deep Neural Networks
from Simulation to the Real World", IROS 2017 / arXiv 1703.06907.
- Trained an object detector on simulated images only, with non-realistic random textures; localizes real
  objects to within **1.5 cm** on average, robust to distractors and partial occlusion.
- What was randomized: number and shape of distractor objects; position and texture of all objects;
  textures of table, floor, skybox and robot; position, orientation and field of view of the camera;
  number of lights, their position/orientation/specular; type and amount of image noise.
- Camera: each sample placed within a **10 x 5 x 10 cm box** around a hand-placed initial point that
  approximately matches the real camera; viewing angle offset by up to 0.1 rad; field of view scaled up to 5%.
- Between 0 and 10 distractors per scene.
- Training size: relatively accurate with as few as **5,000** samples (pre-trained), improving up to about
  **50,000**. Performance degrades significantly below **1,000** unique textures (at 10,000 images).
- Ablation Table II (detection error, cm, object only / distractors / occlusions): full method 1.3 / 1.8 /
  2.4; no camera randomization **2.0 / 2.4 / 2.9**; no distractors in training 1.5 / **7.2 / 7.4**; no noise
  1.4 / 1.9 / 2.4. Paper: camera randomization "consistently provides a slight accuracy boost"; distractors
  "critical"; noise "negligible".
- Still over-fits sim: sim error 0.3 to 0.5 cm vs 1.5 cm real (a sim-to-real gap stated by the authors).
- No real data at training time, so "no validation on real data can be done during training".
- Robot test: detected and picked the target in **38 of 40** trials in cluttered scenes.
- **Derived:** Wilson 95% for 38/40 = 0.835 to 0.986 (engine `wilson(38, 40)`; not quoted unless printed).

### F2. Over-randomization and uniform sampling (Mehta et al. 2019, read at source, abstract)
Mehta, Diaz, Golemo, Pal, Paull, "Active Domain Randomization", arXiv 1904.04762 (CoRL 2019). Abstract:
domain randomization "may lead to suboptimal, high-variance policies", which they attribute to uniform
sampling of environment parameters. Used to ground the anti-lever as a known concern; teach it as an open
question (how wide is wide enough is not settled; Tobin found more textures helped up to a point).

### F3. Teleoperation and action chunking (Zhao, Kumar, Levine, Finn 2023, read at source, full text)
"Learning Fine-Grained Bimanual Manipulation with Low-Cost Hardware", arXiv 2304.13705 (RSS 2023). ALOHA
system and ACT (Action Chunking with Transformers).
- Whole teleoperation system costs **under $20k** with off-the-shelf parts.
- Control and data recording at **50 Hz**. Episodes take **8 to 14 seconds**: 400 to 700 time steps.
- **50 demonstrations per task** (100 for Thread Velcro); about **10 to 20 minutes** of data per task and
  **30 to 60 minutes wall-clock** because of resets and teleoperator mistakes.
- Actions recorded are the **leader** arm joint positions, not the follower's: force is implicit in the
  difference between them through the low-level controller.
- Observations: follower joint positions plus **4 cameras**.
- Human demonstrations are "inherently stochastic" even from one person (the handover position differs
  every episode).
- Abstract: six real tasks, **80 to 90%** success, from **10 minutes** of demonstrations.
- Real-world tasks evaluated with **25 trials** (one seed); simulated with 3 seeds x 50 trials.
- Chunking ablation: success **1% at k = 1 to 44% at k = 100**, slight dip at k = 200, 400 (close to
  open loop); averaged across 4 settings (2 simulated tasks x scripted or human data), temporal ensemble
  disabled. Two baselines augmented with chunking showed the same trend. Temporal ensemble: +3.3% for
  ACT, +4% for a baseline.
- Training without the CVAE objective on human data: **35.3% to 2%**.
- User study (6 participants): 50 Hz vs 5 Hz; zip tie 33 s at 5 Hz vs 20 s at 50 Hz; cups 16 s vs 10 s;
  5 Hz is a **62%** increase in teleoperation time.

### F4. Open X-Embodiment (Open X-Embodiment Collaboration 2023, read at source, full text v-latest)
"Open X-Embodiment: Robotic Learning Datasets and RT-X Models", arXiv 2310.08864 (ICRA 2024).
- **1M+ real robot trajectories**, **22 robot embodiments**, **21 institutions**, **60 datasets**,
  **527 skills (160,266 tasks)**.
- Coarse alignment: a 7-dimensional end-effector action (x, y, z, roll, pitch, yaw, gripper); one canonical
  camera view per dataset resized to a common resolution; each dataset's actions normalized before
  discretization, de-normalized per embodiment.
- Caveats stated by the authors: camera observations "still vary substantially" (camera poses and
  properties); "we do not align the coordinate frames across datasets"; actions may be absolute or relative
  positions or velocities; "the same action vector may induce very different motions for different robots."
- RT-1-X mean success **50% higher** than the original method or RT-1 on small-data domains.
- On large-scale datasets RT-1-X **underfits and performs worse** than the original method and RT-1 (the
  disagreement to teach: mixing helps the small, can hurt the large at small model size).
- RT-2-X emergent skills about **3x** RT-2.

### F5. DROID (Khazatsky et al. 2024, read at source, abstract + HTML body)
"DROID: A Large-Scale In-The-Wild Robot Manipulation Dataset", arXiv 2403.12945 (RSS 2024).
- **Derived (a2):** 350 h / 76k is about 17 s per trajectory; 350 h / 50 collectors is about 7 h each.
- **76k** demonstration trajectories, about **350 hours**, **564 scenes**, **50 data collectors**,
  **12 months**, North America, Asia and Europe. (Task count differs between the abstract and the body: do
  not quote it.)
- Same hardware setup replicated **18** times; three cameras: two exterior on tripods, one on the wrist.
- Extrinsics: initial calibration with a checkerboard; a later automatic calibration with quality metrics.
- Collectors mark each episode success or failure; about **16k** unsuccessful trajectories released
  separately; failed episodes excluded from policy training.
- Protocol aims to prevent "camera cannot see robot" and "teleoperator in camera view".
- Policy evaluation: **10 rollouts per task setting per method**, A/B.

### F6. Policy evaluation practice (Kress-Gazit et al. 2024, read at source, HTML)
"Robot Learning as an Empirical Science: Best Practices for Policy Evaluation", arXiv 2409.09491.
- Papers usually report success rate with little on number of runs, initial conditions, success criteria or
  statistical analysis; recommend reporting conditions, complementary metrics, statistical analysis, and
  qualitative failure modes.
- "a clear, detailed, and unambiguous definition of success"; the behavior designer writes the criteria but
  is not the evaluator.
- Interleave policy rollouts blind to the evaluator; evaluate all policies in one session; match initial
  conditions with image overlays or markings.
- Example: A 13/20 vs B 14/20, "we cannot state that one policy outperforms the other".
- Example: re-running policy A on the same initial conditions after a lab reorganization dropped success
  from **13/20 to 0/8** (distribution shift).
- Uses Bayesian analysis (uniform prior) in examples; mentions frequentist intervals as the alternative.

### F7. Wilson score interval (Wilson 1927, reported) and Fisher's exact test (standard)
E. B. Wilson, "Probable Inference, the Law of Succession, and Statistical Inference", Journal of the
American Statistical Association 22 (158): 209-212, 1927. Reported tier (paywalled; the formula is
standard and implemented in the engine). Fisher's exact test: standard; engine matches scipy to 1e-9.
- **Derived (engine, printed by the p7 widget):** Wilson 95% for 18/20 = **0.699 to 0.972**; for 15/20 =
  **0.531 to 0.888**; for 0/20 = **0 to 0.161**; for 20/20 = **0.839 to 1**. Fisher two-sided 18/20 vs
  15/20: **p = 0.4075**; 18/20 vs 5/20: p = 0.00007.
- Teach: the Wald interval collapses to zero width at 0/n and n/n; Wilson does not.

### F8. Collaborative robot safety standards (reported)
- ISO/TS 15066:2016 (paywalled, reported): power and force limiting is one of four collaborative operation
  modes; Annex A tabulates maximum contact force and pressure per body region, set from pain-onset
  thresholds; values described as preliminary. No per-region number is quoted on any page.
- Behrens et al., Frontiers in Robotics and AI 2022, doi 10.3389/frobt.2021.667818 (reported from the
  abstract snippet): a study with **112** subjects toward replacing those values; pain thresholds differ
  between men and women in some body regions.
- ISO 10218-1 and -2, 2025 revision (reported, trade press and the standards body's partners): collaborative
  content formerly in ISO/TS 15066 is consolidated into Part 1; functional safety made explicit;
  cybersecurity added as it affects safety. Re-verify the catalogue before delivery.
- Teach as data: protective stops and force-limit trips are logged events with a cost; never as legal
  advice or a compliance checklist.

## 3. The bench (p10) - canon, computed in node AND Python (agree on 1272 fields, 2026-11-22)

Engine `assets/pai-live.js`; mirror `materials/pai_reference.py`; canon JSON `materials/pai_canon.json`
(`node materials/pai_canon.js . materials/pai_canon.json`, then
`python3 -I materials/pai_reference.py --compare materials/pai_canon.json`; a planted 0.1% fault fails it).
Everything synthetic and constructed. Policy: ridge regression x = b0 + b1 u + b2 e + b3 g on 400
simulated episodes; a grasp succeeds within 1.0 cm. Real cell: 50 trials (canon); a 2,000-trial "truth"
set only a constructed world can have. Seed 1122.

- Per condition, N=50 (dim = light below 0.85, 33 trials; normal 17; centre |x| <= 5 cm, 29; edge 21):
  - rung 1: dim 17/33, normal 12/17, centre 18/29, edge 11/21
  - rung 2: dim 25/33, normal 12/17, centre 23/29, edge 14/21
  - rung 3: dim 30/33, normal 16/17, centre 29/29, **edge 17/21** (the lens error only real data shows)
  - rung 4: dim 32/33, normal 17/17, centre 29/29, **edge 20/21**
  - over: dim 17/33, **normal 2/17**, centre 7/29, edge 12/21 (the straight-line fit to a curved
    lighting effect fails in ordinary light)
- Coefficients: rung 3 b2 = -0.994 (the policy learned to subtract the fiducial); rung 1 b2 = 0.016.
- Fisher two-sided, rung 4 vs rung 1 at N=50: p < 0.0001 (9.4e-7).
- N = 20 (same seed): rung 2 18/20, rung 3 19/20, rung 4 20/20 (0.839-1): three rungs the 20-trial
  eval cannot tell apart; truth differs (0.716 / 0.908 / 0.951). The p7 lesson in the bench itself.
- N = 200: 130, 147, 190, 197; over 64; break 1/200.
- Other seeds (N=50) for "your numbers will differ": seed 1: 26, 38, 46, 48; seed 7: 34, 34, 45, 48;
  seed 42: 35, 43, 46, 48. Shape holds; rung 1 vs 2 can tie at 50 trials (seed 7).
- selfTest: 5 properties (Wilson 0/20 upper = 0.1611; ridge recovers known coefficients; Fisher 18/20 vs
  15/20 = 0.4075; camera randomization gives b2 near -1; the unrecorded bump drops success by 40+ points),
  each failing on its planted fault (wilsonWald, ridgeTranspose, fisherPointOnly, simCamIgnored,
  bumpModelled).
- Declared modelled on the widget: object range +-10 cm, real camera N(0.2, 0.7) cm, real lighting
  U(0.6, 1.1), lighting bias 5d + 5d^2, noise 0.2 + d^2, lens 1.08, tolerance 1.0 cm.

Reading (teach it this way): in this constructed world ANY nonzero camera width teaches the policy to subtract
the fiducial (the camera effect is linear); widening the camera further neither helps nor hurts. The harm in
the anti-lever comes from the LIGHTING width, because the lighting effect is curved and the policy is a
straight line. Lighting 0.2 already reaches 46 with the camera on. Real policies and real effects differ;
the lesson is the mechanism (capacity spent on conditions that never occur), not the numbers.

### Other derived values (engine `PAI.wilson` / `PAI.fisher`; quote only where a widget prints them or as derived)
- Wilson 95%: 38/40 = 0.835-0.986 (Tobin's grasps); 25/25 = 0.867-1; 9/10 = 0.596-0.982; 13/20 = 0.433-0.819;
  14/20 = 0.481-0.855. Fisher two-sided 13/20 vs 14/20: p = 1.0 (Kress-Gazit's energy-bar example).
- Calibration geometry (derived, p2): a 1 degree camera yaw error at 60 cm working distance moves the
  apparent point by 600 x tan(1 deg) = 10.47 mm, about 1.05 cm, more than Wrenfield's 1.0 cm tolerance.

## 4. Per-session coverage

Leader (no code):
- **a1 The robot is a data loop** - the loop: sense, policy, act, log, learn; what an episode is; where the
  data team sits; F1 (1.5 cm, 38/40), F3 (50 Hz, 10 min of demos), F4 headline; the bench ladder previewed.
  Five questions to put to any robot data claim. Not investment advice.
- **a2 Demonstrations are the dataset** - F3 (cost of a demo in wall-clock, leader vs follower), F5
  (76k, 350 h, 50 collectors, failed episodes), F4 scale; demos as the expensive label; who collects.
- **a3 What a success rate can and cannot tell you** - F6, F7 (18/20 vs 15/20, Wilson), ACT 25 trials;
  the 20-trial bench row (18, 19, 20 of 20).
- **a4 Reading a robot demo claim** - speed-ups (playback rate), cuts, hidden teleoperation, cherry-picked
  takes, success criteria; F6 (designer is not evaluator, blind interleaving), F6 13/20 -> 0/8.
- **a5 Why the warehouse playbook breaks here** - source (sensors, not people), identity (episode + robot +
  calibration), ground truth (success defined by a person), feedback loop (policy changes the data it
  collects), cost of being wrong (a moving arm near people, F8).
- **a6 From warehouse to physical AI: the transfer map** - carries over / must learn / must unlearn;
  90-day ladder with official/free sources (the papers above, the ISO catalogue page, open dataset docs).

Practitioner:
- **p1 Anatomy of an episode** - observations, actions, timesteps, success flag, metadata; F3 (50 Hz,
  400-700 steps, 4 cameras), F5 (3 cameras, success/failure marking).
- **p2 Coordinate frames and calibration** - base, camera, end-effector, object frames; extrinsics; F5
  checkerboard + automatic calibration; F4 "we do not align the coordinate frames"; bench break button.
- **p3 Teleop data quality** - F3 (stochastic demos, 30-60 min wall-clock, mistakes), F5 (failed episodes,
  "teleoperator in camera view"); filtering, labelling success, operator variance.
- **p4 Mixing data across robots** - F4 (7-D action, normalization, 50% higher small-data, underfits on
  large); embodiment; teach the disagreement.
- **p5 Actions as data** - absolute vs delta, joint vs end-effector, normalization statistics, chunks;
  F3 chunking 1% -> 44%, temporal ensemble, leader joint positions.
- **p6 Domain randomization** - F1 in full, F2; widths as hyperparameters; bench rungs 1-3 and the anti-lever.
- **p7 Evaluating a policy in 20 trials** - F7 numbers, F6 practices; paired conditions (same initial
  conditions), Fisher, why 18/20 vs 15/20 may not differ; bench N=20 row.
- **p8 The intervention log as labels** - human takeovers and corrections during rollouts as labels for the
  next round (constructed Wrenfield log); selection bias of interventions; links to learn-autonomous-driving
  for disengagements (seam).
- **p9 Shared space and stop logs** - F8 (reported), protective stops and force-limit trips as data, the
  cost asymmetry near people, stop-rate denominators (per hour of shared operation, per pick).
- **p10 The sim-to-real bench** - section 3.
